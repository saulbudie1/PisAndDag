# PisAndDag — Timeline

> Companion to `Terraria_Mod_Lore_Bible_ver_1.1.md`. The Bible holds what is true. This
> holds when it happened, and in what order.
>
> There is one hard number in the book: **three hundred years, give or take**, from the
> Archmage's working to the player's arrival (§XIV). Everything below is placed against
> that one event, which is called **the Working** here and is treated as year zero.
>
> No dates are given. Nobody in the world keeps a calendar that survived, and the one man
> who keeps records counts deaths rather than years (§XIV, the Clerk). Anything the Bible
> does not fix is marked **[OPEN]** and must not be quietly settled in dialogue.

---

## 1. Before reckoning

Order known, distance unknown. These are not three hundred years ago; they are simply
before.

- Mors remembers every death and performs the ending of each one. Death is not cruel, it
  is final (§VIII).
- The God of Birth steals one of the Death Daggers. It works for her, because she is the
  maker of what she ends (§VII, §XVI).
- The Low Court's attempt on the Jungle. **Resolved:** this is before the Archmage, and
  everything the Jungle has done since is one uninterrupted reaction (§XVII, planning 1.5).
- The learned houses study the three infectious forces closely enough to bind them, lay
  the Seal, and write the key into a bloodline so that someone, one day, could open the
  lock (§XXVII).
- The Archmage is brought in as the outside scholar on that work. He leaves carrying the
  method rather than the doctrine: that an unkillable thing is not out-fought but
  un-ruled, and that a person can be a container (§XIII).

---

## 2. The last century in which death worked

Everything in this part happens while dying still ends a life. It is the only part of the
timeline where that is true, and the player never sees it — they only ever walk through
what it left.

### The Kingdom of Grace at its height

The royal civilization, its legitimacy inseparable from divine favour. **[OPEN]** whether
that favour was Shimmer misread or a deliberate lie (§XIX).

### The Arcanum tells Bustworth what is coming

A lord of Grace named **Bustworth** holds a standing connection to the Arcanum (§XXIV).
Somebody inside it knows what the Archmage is building and tells him the world is about to
stop keeping score.

**[OPEN]** Whether that warning was a gift, a purchase, or a use somebody was putting him
to. It decides whether the Arcanum is a party to what follows.

### The patricide

Bustworth kills his father, **before the Working**, knowing the Working is coming (§XIX).

A crown taken after it could be reclaimed by the man it was taken from. Taken before, it
could not. His is one of the last killings in the world that held.

### Bustworth, Lord of Grace

He takes the kingdom, declines the kingship he cannot claim under a word like Grace, and
gives himself the title he can: Lord (§XIX).

The kingdom prospers. He is clever, formidable, and by every measure that is not
inheritance the best ruler Grace ever had.

### The goblins arrive

The Goblin Empire, out of the Artificer tradition, and their engineering is better than
Grace's (§XXI). The war begins here.

**[OPEN — the single most load-bearing open question in this document]** Whether the war
and the goblins' experimental programme run before the Working, after it, or straddle it.

- **Straddling is the recommended reading.** The war starts as a war and becomes something
  else on one particular day, and the record of the programme changes tone on that day
  without stopping. It also explains why Grace's fall has no date: it stopped being
  losable.
- If the programme is entirely **after**, the goblins had subjects who could not die, and
  the work had no limit of any kind.
- If entirely **before**, the atrocity is bounded and the war is winnable, which costs the
  stalemate and most of what makes Bustworth readable.

## 3. The Working — year zero

The Archmage erases Death's memory (§VIII, §XIII).

He is not trying to kill Mors, which was never available to anyone. He wounds what cannot
be ended and leaves it running.

From this moment: mortals return, decay does not land, the town comes back from goblin
arrows, and nothing in the world finishes except in the fungal caves (§XIV, §XXVIII).

The Archmage survives it and is still alive at the player's arrival, the only person who
remembers death working (§XIII).

---

## 4. The three hundred years

The long middle. Nobody alive at the end of it has known anything else.

- **Grace bleeds out.** Not a defeat with a date. A war that could no longer be lost, under
  a lord who would not concede it (§XIX). The kingdom is ruins before the Low Court
  convenes (§XIV).
- **[OPEN] Bustworth's fate.** The Bible locks that the Archmage is *the only person in the
  world who remembers death working* (§XIII). That line and a surviving Bustworth cannot
  both stand. Three ways out, none chosen:
  1. **He died before the Working** — in the war's early years, while dying still ended
     things. The only Lord of Grace who cannot come back, and the war outlives him by
     centuries because nobody was left with the authority to call it off.
  2. **He is alive and does not remember it working** — three hundred years of a mind under
     the amnesty, and the one man who knew what he was spending no longer knows he spent
     it. §XIII survives intact. This is the strongest version and it rhymes with Mors
     without repeating him.
  3. **§XIII is amended.** Cleanest to write and the most expensive; that line is doing
     real work for the Archmage.
- **The Low Court convenes** and inherits a library it will later lose (§XIV, §XVII).
  **[OPEN]** whether their divine anatomy descends from the goblin notes; the method was
  written down on people first and somebody found it.
- **The Clerk** begins recording every death in the world and does not stop (§XIV).
- **Nine Tenths** sets up as his own stock (§XIV).
- **The Kingdom of Grace** is a ruin the Court convened in the shadow of.
- **The Mechanic and the Goblin Tinkerer**, much later, at the end of this stretch. He is
  found bound in a cave; his army says it was for losing a battle (§XXI).
- **The un-rotted, the recut stone, the overstayed** accumulate across the whole period and
  are the only visible evidence any of this happened (§XIV).

---

## 5. Now

The player arrives. First death, first burial, the open grave in the shoreline graveyard
takes it and no other (§XXXII.3).

The Blade of Mors has been standing in the ash at the bottom of the world for three hundred
years, and the man who put it there has walked past it since without knowing (§XVI).
