# The Kingdom of Grace — Structure Plan

> Companion to `Terraria_Mod_Lore_Bible_ver_1.1.md` (§XIX Grace, §XX the Princess,
> §XXI the goblin empire, §XIV the two eras of death) and `PAD_World_Plan.md`, which
> places this on the map.
>
> Scale target: Leyndell. Not in tile count — in the number of times the player has to
> stop walking and look up.
>
> Everything here is **[PROPOSAL]** unless it quotes a **[LOCKED]** line from the Bible.
> Nothing below settles Bustworth's fate; the timeline holds that open and so does this.

---

## 1. The one idea

§XIX locks it in a sentence:

> The ruins the player walks through are not a defeat. They are a stalemate that nobody
> was ever allowed to call.

So the capital is **not sacked**. It is not burned, not breached, not looted. The walls
stand. The gates are shut. The granaries are full of nine-hundred-year-old grain that has
not spoiled, because nothing in this era finishes (§XIV). The goblin engines outside are
still pointed at it. The siege is, in the narrowest sense, ongoing.

The player is walking into an argument that both sides are still winning.

Everything in the build serves that. **No rubble where a story would put rubble.** The
damage is all in the wrong places: fine where the siege was, ruinous where it wasn't.

### The second idea, which is the order of the rooms

§XIX also locks this, and it decides the geography:

> The player should meet the prosperity before the murder.

The plain runs west to east and so does the reading. Roads and grain first. The court
last. The player should like the place for twenty minutes before anything tells them what
it cost.

For comparison: a vanilla Dungeon is about 250 tiles across. Grace is **seven of them
end to end**, and stands about as tall above the ground as the Dungeon runs below it.

## 3. The quarters, and the ground before them

### −1 · The Cleared Ground — x 9440 – 10330

Not part of the capital. The reason the capital lands.

Eight hundred and ninety tiles of open, flat, felled ground between the jungle and the
siege works, and the Curtain standing over the far end of it the whole way across. An
army clears its field of fire before it settles in, and this one did. Stumps. Spoil.
Old cut lines. No standing timber, three hundred years on, because nothing in this era
finishes and that includes a clearance.

Nothing is placed here and nothing needs to be. The walk is the content.

**Loot tier:** none. Deep below it, y 1400 – 2200, is the Sealed Pocket
(`PAD_World_Plan.md` §4), which the player has no way into and every reason to remember
once the Wall is dead.

---

### 0 · The Wasted Mile — x 10330 – 10800

Outside the wall. Goblin ground.

- **Engine frames.** Four of them, 40 tiles tall, timber and iron, still loaded. Not
  wrecked. Parked.
- **The trench lines.** Three parallel cuts running the height of the plain edge, dug
  into the dirt layer, roofed with plate. They connect to the Undercity. This is how it
  was actually done, and the player finds it before they find the wall that didn't
  matter.
- **The bore.** A single machine, half buried, its head pointed down and east. It is
  still turning, one tooth every few seconds. The sound carries across the plain.
- **The tally-plate facility** (§XXI [PROPOSAL], adopted here). One room off the second
  trench. Bench, restraints, drainage, notes. The plate on the wall lists subjects by
  number; one number is struck through and rewritten underneath itself, over and over,
  in the same careful hand. The player has already seen that shape on a recut gravestone
  in a churchyard and felt sorry about it.
  - Nothing in the room is labelled. No sign says what it was for.
- **The Low Court camp.** §XIV has the Court convene in the shadow of the ruins. Put the
  camp here, at the near edge, tents pitched on the goblin works rather than the
  kingdom's, which tells the player something about the Court's manners.

**Loot tier:** none. One lore item.

---

### 1 · The Curtain — x 10800 – 11020

The outer wall. 160 tiles tall, 40 thick, unbroken along its whole length.

- **There is no way through it.** No gate the player can open, no collapsed section, no
  crack. The one entrance is a goblin bore-hole 30 tiles below the plain, which means
  the player's first act at Grace is to go **under** the thing that held.
- Faced in pale stone with gold inlay at the crown, intact, because nothing weathers
  properly any more.
- Two inscriptions, one on each face, and they do not agree:

  > *Held on the ninth day. Held on the ninetieth.* — outer face, near the bore-hole
  >
  > *The stone was never breached.* — inner face, at head height, cut later

**Build note:** the Curtain should be visible from the far side of the Jungle. Its crown
sits at y 300, above the surface line of every biome to its west.

---

### 2 · The Processional — x 11020 – 11480

A raised avenue, 12 tiles above the plain, running dead straight to the Low City gate.
Statue colonnade both sides, forty plinths.

- Every statue is intact. Every face is intact. Nothing has been thrown down.
- Each plinth carries a name and a number: *Second of the name. Buried in the spring.*
  and so on down the line, getting more recent as the player walks east.
- **The last plinth is newer stone and it does not match.** It carries two words:

  > *Lord of Grace.*

  No name. No number. The player will not know for hours why that is the loudest thing
  on the road.

**Loot tier:** none. This is a corridor that exists to be walked slowly.

---

### 3 · The Low City — x 11480 – 11850

The good years, standing up.

This is the section that has to be **pleasant**. Not haunting. A town the player would
want to live in.

- **The granaries.** Six of them, stone, sixty tiles tall, and they are full. Grain nine
  hundred years old and perfectly good. Door lintel:

  > *Three years of it, against a year of need.*

- **The waterworks.** An aqueduct entering from the north wall, a cistern, and clay pipe
  running under every street to standpipes at each corner. It still runs. The player can
  drink from it.
- **The almshouses.** Small, well built, warm colours, glass in the windows. Bunks,
  hearths, a kitchen per block. Nobody in them.
- **The road itself.** Cut stone, cambered, with drains. §XIX: *roads, grain, order,
  work.* Say it with masonry, not with a sign.
- **The market hall.** Stalls, scales, weights, a posted table of fair prices. The
  weights are honest — a player who checks will find they are.

**Loot tier 0** (open from the start): utility and flavour. Food that never spoils, a
set of honest scales, a lore item from the almshouse.

**The turn.** At the east end of the market hall, one door in the floor, locked from the
other side. It goes to the Undercity. It is the only unpleasant thing in the quarter and
the player should find it last.

---

### 4 · The High Ward — x 11850 – 12220

The court. Sealed.

- **The Ward Gate.** Shut and will not open. It needs a key that is not lying about
  (see §6, Gating). Until then the player can walk the rampart above and look down into
  the courtyard, which is the correct way to first see this place.
- **The Court of Grace.** One room, 200 tiles wide, 90 tall. Gold floor, a long table
  down the middle, forty-one chairs. Carved at the head of the table, where a man would
  read it every time he sat:

  > *May I say grace?*

  (§XIX [LOCKED]. This is the whole character in four words and it goes on a wall, not
  in a book.)
- **The throne.** Raised, plain, smaller than every visual in the room would lead the
  player to expect. Whether anything is on it is **[OPEN]** — see §7.
- **The Princess's rooms** (§XX). Untouched. Bed made. Books on divine legitimacy and
  sacred bloodline doctrine with a child's annotations in the margins, and one of the
  annotations is a question mark.
- **The armoury.** Grace's own war gear: ceremonial, beautiful, and visibly worse than
  the goblin equipment the player crossed to get here. That is the thesis as a loot
  table — *steel over blessing, workmanship over legitimacy* (§XIX).

**Loot tier 2.** A weapon, an accessory, the Princess hook.

---

### 5 · The Font — x 12220 – 12450

What the favour actually was.

- A chamber below the chapel, and from it one cut shaft going down — a long way down — to
  the only seam of Shimmer in the world, which comes up under this kingdom and under no
  other part of it (`PAD_World_Plan.md` §4a). They did not find it and then build here.
  They built here because it was here.
- **What is down there now is water.** §XI: the Archmage locked what Shimmer *does*, not
  where it is. It was not always water. The good years had the live thing and the
  bloodline is what the live thing made of them; the lock came after, and nobody up here
  was told, and the service carried on.
- So the Font is full of **vessels** — ewers, jugs, a stoppered reservoir in the cistern
  room, bottles in the Reliquary with the seals still on. A liquid that changes what
  things are cannot be decanted. This one can, and the whole apparatus for decanting it is
  the loudest thing in the room.
- The shaft's length is whatever that world's Shimmer roll gave, so the walk from the
  chapel to the water is a different length every time. Three hundred years of carrying it
  further than anybody remembers agreeing to.
- Ladles. Ewers. A service trolley. A procession route marked on the floor, from the
  shaft head to the Court of Grace, worn into the stone by three centuries of feet.
- Inscription at the shaft head:

  > *Drawn here. Carried to the table. None asked what it was.*

- **The room is a boss arena.** §XIX, *The Cupbearer*: the small gold dragon who walked
  that procession route is still down here, and this is the first fight after the Moon
  Lord. It changes nothing above — the chamber is built as a service room and reads as one
  — but it sets a floor on the clear span, keeps the trolley out of the middle, and means
  the way in wants to be one door.
- **The shaft head is under the arena, and the floor over it burns.** She ends the fight
  by falling down her own shaft into the pool, so the mouth has to be inside the room, in
  the floor, covered, and the covering has to be something her fire takes away over the
  length of a fight. Build it as floor, not as a hole with a lid. Everything the player
  needs to understand afterwards is that the way down was under them the whole time.
- **The room after.** Burnt floor, a hole in it, the shaft open, and the trolley still
  standing with the one ewer she poured before the fight, still full. No body, and nothing
  dropped on the ground. Whatever she leaves, she does not leave it lying there.
- **Say nothing else.** §XIX marks the Shimmer reading **[PROPOSAL / DEVELOPING]** and
  leaves open whether it was a misunderstanding or a deliberate lie. The room must
  support both and state neither. No document in it explains, no NPC comments.

**Loot tier 2.** One lore item. No power.

---

### 6 · The Dry Shore — x 12450 – 12600

The east edge of the world, where the ocean would be in any other world and is not
(`PAD_World_Plan.md` §3).

- A sea wall, a harbour, quays, bollards, capstans, a lighthouse — all correct, all
  finished, all facing 150 tiles of dry rock and a cliff.
- Ships in the harbour basin, sitting on their keels in dust.
- This is the one place where the player is told something by absence: the kingdom built
  for a sea that the world does not have. No line explains it. **[OPEN]** whether the
  sea left, was never there, or is somewhere else now.

**Loot tier 1.** Fishing gear that has never been wet. One lore item.

## 4. Beneath

### The Undercity — y 520 – 1040, full width

Where the population went.

- The Low City's streets repeated underground, one storey down, but built in a hurry:
  same plan, worse masonry, no windows because there is nothing to look out of.
- Bunks by the thousand. Kitchens. A hospital. A school.
- The goblin trenches (quarter 0) break into it from the west, and the breaks are old and
  have been fought over and fought over again.
- Inscription, in a stairwell, in a clerk's hand:

  > *Counted out at the gate. Counted back at dusk.*

  And further in, later, the same hand, and that is the whole of it:

  > *Counted out.*

**Loot tier 1.** Mid-game. Dense, fought-for, worth doing.

### The Long Reliquary — y 1040 – 1500, x 11400 – 12400

The kingdom's own record of itself: regalia, bloodline rolls, the accounts of the good
years, and one shelf of them that has been very carefully edited.

**Loot tier 3.** Hardmode.

### The Rootworks — the Shimmer's own cavern, wherever it came out

The world's one pool of Shimmer, and the reason for all of the above.

The capital is built **around** it rather than the pool placed under the capital: the
Grace passes run after the Shimmer pass and read where it landed, which — with the
Dungeon forced to the left — is always somewhere in the right seventh of the world
(`PAD_World_Plan.md` §4a). Roughly y 1500 – 1900, x 11214 – 12400, and it moves.

The shaft from the Font comes down into it. So does something else, older, from a
different direction, and the Bible does not say who cut that one.

**Loot tier 3.** Hardmode. One lore item, and it is the one that makes §5 legible.

## 5. Build rules

1. **No rubble where the siege was.** The Curtain is perfect. The Wasted Mile is a
   working site. The ruin is inward: the Undercity, the Reliquary, the places nobody was
   shooting at.
2. **Nothing is labelled.** §XXI's rule for the goblin facility applies to the whole
   capital. Signs say prices and names, never purposes.
3. **Gold is structural, not decorative.** Inlay at load-bearing lines only. The moment
   it reads as treasure, the place reads as a dungeon.
4. **Two inscriptions per quarter, maximum.** They are fragments, third person, past
   tense, and at least one pair in the build contradicts another pair.
5. **Every room the player can stand in was a room somebody worked in.** No corridors
   that exist to be corridors.
6. **The vertical is the point.** The player should have to look up at least six times:
   the engine frames, the Curtain, the granaries, the Ward Gate from below, the Court
   ceiling, the lighthouse.

---

## 6. Gating

Grace sits at the far right of the world and a determined player can walk there on day
one. The gate is internal, physical, and never a message box.

| Tier | Opens | Reached by |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Wasted Mile, Curtain bore-hole, Processional, Low City | walking |
| 1 | Undercity, Dry Shore | the trapdoor in the market hall, which is locked from below — so: through the goblin trenches first |
| 2 | High Ward, the Font | the Ward Gate, which needs a key **[OPEN — what]** |
| 3 | Long Reliquary, Rootworks | after the Seal breaks |

**[OPEN] The Ward Gate key.** Three candidates, none chosen:
1. Something the Princess has and does not know she has.
2. Something in the goblin facility — they got in, so they had one.
3. Nothing. The gate opens when the world changes, and the player never learns why.

---

## 7. What this plan deliberately does not decide

- **Bustworth's fate.** `PAD_Timeline.md` §4 holds three readings open. The throne is
  built either way; what is on it is a switch, not a design.
  - *Died before the Working* → the throne is empty and there is a grave in the
    Reliquary with one date on it, the only such stone in the world.
  - *Alive and does not remember* → he is somewhere, and not here.
  - *§XIII amended* → he is on the throne and the player can talk to him.
- **Whether the Grace was Shimmer or a lie.** The Font supports both.
- **Where the sea went.** The Dry Shore asks; nothing answers.
- **Whether the war is over.** Nothing in the build says it is.
